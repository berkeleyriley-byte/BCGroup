Tokenomics

The Atomic Unit of AI Value

Tokenomics, or AI Token Economics, is the emerging discipline of understanding, measuring, and optimizing the cost of AI. Tokens—the atomic units of data processed by large language models—are the fundamental measure of AI consumption. Traditional cloud cost management falls short because AI expenses are highly variable and tied directly to token usage, not just fixed infrastructure. Tokenomics bridges this gap, acting as "FinOps for AI." It involves tracking not just raw token volume (throughput), but "goodput"—tokens that produce useful, valuable outputs. By understanding the cost per token and mapping it to specific business outcomes, organizations can align AI investments with actual value creation, moving beyond infrastructure metrics to manage the true variable cost of intelligence.

Methodology

Drive Down Token Use and Cost

Optimizing AI costs requires a strategic methodology focused on engineering levers rather than just procurement. A key strategy is model routing: not every task requires the most expensive, frontier model. Teams can significantly cut costs by routing complex tasks to advanced models while handling routine queries with smaller, cheaper, or open-source alternatives. Additionally, semantic caching—storing and reusing responses for similar queries—prevents redundant processing, driving down token usage. Reducing system prompt overhead and minimizing retry storms are also crucial. The goal is to maximize "goodput" while minimizing unnecessary processing. By implementing smart routing, caching, and efficient prompt design, businesses can achieve the required performance at a fraction of the cost, making their AI operations sustainable and scalable.

03

Performance

Measuring Outcomes and ROI

Measuring AI performance in the context of Tokenomics goes beyond simple latency; it's about connecting cost to business value. Key metrics include "Token consumption efficiency" (evaluating cost against output usefulness) and "Token yield rate" (the percentage of generated tokens that actually contribute to a downstream business action, factoring in retries and failed outputs). We also evaluate the "cost per inference" and overall "inference efficiency," which links the cost of an AI event to the outcome it produced. As the AI market evolves—with falling raw token costs but rising total usage—tracking these metrics is vital. It's not about minimizing token consumption outright, but ensuring that the tokens consumed translate into a proportional or greater return on investment.

Planning

Take Control of Your AI Spending

Effective AI planning requires taking control of spending before it accelerates uncontrollably. As AI models become increasingly agentic—executing multi-step workflows and generating massive token volumes—governance is critical. Planning involves forecasting AI consumption, allocating token costs to specific business units, and establishing clear accountability. Organizations must be wary of hidden token meters within SaaS subscriptions and strive for visibility across their AI stack. By treating tokens as a strategic business resource, companies can budget accurately, justify AI expenditures, and clearly demonstrate ROI. A proactive planning approach ensures that AI initiatives remain financially viable and aligned with long-term enterprise goals, turning unpredictable costs into managed investments.
